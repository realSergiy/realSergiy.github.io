title: Implantable Hardware Advancements (2023–2029)
---

## TL;DR
- The single biggest near-term shift is the arrival of totally implantable cochlear implants (TICI): Envoy Medical's Acclaim completed full pivotal-trial enrollment (56 patients) on March 11, 2026 — with the final two participants implanted by Dr. Patrick Antonelli at the University of Florida — and targets a PMA submission in Q2 2027, while MED-EL's Mi2000 TICI pivotal trial (NCT06459765) completed in September 2025 and Cochlear is on its third-generation research system — all still investigational, with no firm commercial date except Envoy's CEO-projected late-2027/early-2028 approval.
- Among commercialized internal hardware, Cochlear's FDA-approved (July 8, 2025) Nucleus Nexa is the most consequential: the world's first "smart" implant with upgradeable firmware and internal memory, built on the world's thinnest implant body (3.9 mm); drug-eluting (dexamethasone) electrode arrays from Cochlear and MED-EL are in pivotal trials.
- The most transformative longer-horizon hardware is the optical cochlear implant (micro-LED + optogenetics) from the University of Göttingen/OptoGenTech, which remains preclinical (non-human-primate stage as of 2025) with first-in-human trials still several years away; novel electrode materials (graphene, PEDOT conductive polymers) and implantable microphones (UmboMic) remain at research/preclinical stages.

## Key Findings
The cochlear implant hardware field in 2023–2026 is advancing on two parallel tracks. The **commercial track** is dominated by incremental but meaningful internal-hardware refinement: thinner titanium implant bodies, smarter on-board electronics (Cochlear Nexa), refined MRI-compatible magnets (MED-EL S-Vector), and the first drug-eluting electrode arrays entering pivotal trials. The **research/disruptive track** is pursuing fundamentally new architectures: totally implantable systems (implantable microphones + batteries), optical/optogenetic stimulation, and advanced electrode materials. Most disruptive hardware remains preclinical or in early feasibility, with realistic commercial timelines past 2029.

A consolidation note: Cochlear Ltd completed its acquisition of Oticon Medical's cochlear implant business (the Neuro Zti/Neurelec line) from Demant on May 21, 2024, for zero headline purchase price, ending Oticon Medical as an independent CI developer. Per Cochlear CEO Dig Howitt's May 21, 2024 announcement, the company is committed to "supporting the long-term hearing outcomes of these 20,000 patients" and transitioning the vast majority of Neuro recipients to Cochlear's own platform over time; the Neuro implant line is being wound down rather than further developed.

### Cross-cutting observations
- **Totally implantable systems are the defining race.** Three companies — Cochlear, MED-EL, and Envoy Medical — have human TICI data. The two principal engineering bottlenecks are the implantable microphone (skin/soft-tissue acoustic attenuation, body-noise pickup) and implantable power (battery life and recharging). Cochlear's feasibility study explicitly identified a microphone fluid-ingress failure in 2 of 10 patients, [nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12904232/) underscoring hermetic-sealing challenges. Envoy sidesteps the implantable-microphone problem entirely by sensing incus vibration with a piezoelectric sensor and placing its battery in the chest [The Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/hearing-products/implants-bone-conduction/cochlear-implants/on-the-cusp-of-completely-implantable-cochlear-implants) — an architecturally distinct approach.
- **"Smart" implant electronics** (Cochlear Nexa) mark the first time the internal device — not just the external processor — is upgradeable, which is a genuine hardware-platform shift even though the upgradeability itself is firmware-delivered.
- **Drug-eluting electrodes** are the closest disruptive electrode innovation to market, with both Cochlear (CI632D) and MED-EL (CIDEXEL) in pivotal trials.
- **Optical/optogenetic stimulation** is the most scientifically transformative but remains preclinical, gated by both hardware (µLED arrays, thermal management, power) and biology (gene therapy safety/durability). Commercialization is firmly beyond 2029.

## Recommendations
- **For clinicians/buyers making decisions now (2026):** Adopt commercially available, regulatory-approved internal hardware — Cochlear Nucleus Nexa (smart, upgradeable, thinnest body), MED-EL SYNCHRONY 2 (strongest MRI guarantee), or AB HiRes Ultra 3D (post-redesign). Treat all TICI devices as investigational and do not counsel patients to wait for them outside a trial.
- **For those tracking the field:** The key 2026–2028 inflection points are (1) Envoy Acclaim's PMA submission (target Q2 2027) and any FDA decision — the first realistic fully implanted CI approval; (2) pivotal-trial readouts/approvals for dexamethasone-eluting electrodes (Cochlear CI632D, MED-EL CIDEXEL); and (3) MED-EL Mi2000 TICI regulatory progress following its completed pivotal trial. Benchmarks that would change the outlook: a clean Envoy 12-month pivotal dataset (no serious device failures), or a positive FDA panel, would validate the TICI category; conversely, repeat microphone/hermeticity failures would reset timelines.
- **For investors/strategists:** Watch drug-device convergence (Spiral Therapeutics–AB) and the optogenetics pipeline (OptoGenTech) as the long-horizon disruptors. A first-in-human optical CI trial announcement would be the signal that optogenetic hearing is moving from science to product.

## Caveats
- **Speculative timelines flagged:** Envoy's "late 2027/early 2028" FDA approval and "10–15 year" battery life are CEO projections/design intents, not confirmed outcomes; only the Q2 2027 PMA target and enrollment milestones are company-stated facts. MED-EL and Cochlear have given no firm commercial dates for their TICIs.
- **MED-EL TICI battery chemistry is not publicly disclosed** — do not assume Li-ion by analogy to Cochlear's system.
- **Research-stage items** (UmboMic, optical/optogenetic CI, INS, thin-film/graphene/PEDOT) have no validated commercial timelines and face substantial translation risk; INS feasibility in particular remains scientifically contested.
- The Cochlear TICI is explicitly a "Research System" with a documented microphone failure mode under redesign; it is not a commercial product.
- Some secondary sources (e.g., aggregated "breakthrough innovations" articles) cite specific performance percentages (e.g., "30% signal fidelity improvement" for PEDOT) that could not be independently verified against primary literature and should be treated cautiously.
